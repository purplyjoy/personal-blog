Forgotten Interests

Everyone has things they are interested in. Some people discover their interests early and turn them into hobbies, skills, or even careers. Others try different things until they find something that feels right for them. But not everyone gets the chance to explore everything they are curious about. Sometimes, an interest is left behind because of circumstances. Maybe there wasn't enough money, time, support, or opportunity. Sometimes, we simply grow older and become occupied with other things. What once excited us slowly becomes something we no longer talk about or think about.

I have had many interests like that—things I wanted to try, experience, or become good at, but eventually stopped pursuing. There were not necessarily things I was skilled at. I never got far enough to find out. They were simply things that caught my attention and made me curious about what I could do. But because of different circumstances, many of them remained only as things I wanted to try.

When I had a bicycle as a kid and learned how to ride it, I knew I would have loved cycling. I imagined myself riding with other people, joining biking activities, and perhaps even participating in biking events someday. I loved the feeling of riding—the wind rushing against my face, the wheels rolling beneath me, and the excitement of going faster. I think I would have loved cycling if I had been given the opportunities to do it. But I never really got the chance. I never rode alongside other bikers or became involved in biking activities. All I could do was admire other bikers whenever I saw them on the road. I would watch them ride together, laughing with their friends, and I couldn't help but feel envious. I wanted to experience that too. I wanted to learn the tricks they could do and become comfortable enough on a bike to try them myself. One time, when I was out with my friends, we saw a couple of bikers doing tricks at a park. I remember feeling a pang in my chest as I watched them. I wanted to try it. I wanted to know what it felt like to be able to do those things. But all I could do was imagine.

I've probably said a thousand times that I wanted a big bike. I wanted to ride along the long roads in our city and simply enjoy the feeling of being on a bike. It sounds like such a simple thing, but to me, it was something I had wanted for a long time. But my parents wouldn't allow me. They said it was dangerous to ride a bike, especially as a girl. So, I stopped trying to convince them. I never got to ride with other bikers or experience the things I had imagined. Eventually, that interest became something I left behind—not because I stopped liking it, but because I stopped having the opportunity to pursue it.

Cycling wasn't the only interest I gradually left behind. There were other things I once enjoyed that slowly faded as I grew older. Dancing was one of my favorite things to do at home. I loved moving my body to the music, even though I knew I wasn't particularly good at it. My family encouraged me to dance and participate in dance performances, but not everyone felt the same way. People would sometimes make fun of the way I danced, and over time, their comments made me lose confidence in something I used to enjoy.

But it wasn't like that when I was a kid. Back then, I never really thought about what other people might say or think. If I wanted to dance, I danced. I didn't care whether I looked good or whether anyone thought I was talented. I simply enjoyed it. I even dreamed of someday becoming part of a big dance group, joining competitions, making my family proud, and perhaps even representing our county. I also tried joining a dance club at school, hoping it would help me learn more about dancing and become more comfortable with moving my body. I thought the club would be a place where we could learn, practice, and improve together. However, I quickly noticed that many of the other students were already very talented. Instead of feeling encouraged to improve, I began to feel like I was only comparing myself to them. It felt more like showing what they could already do than trying those who wanted to learn.

Eventually, I lost the motivation to continue. I stopped trying to learn more about dancing, even though I still enjoyed it. I became too conscious of how I looked and how I compared to other dancers. Now, I'm not as energetic as I was when I was younger, and I don't dance as freely as I used to. Sometimes, I wonder if I would still love dancing if I had been given a different environment—one where I could learn without feeling like I had to be good at it first.

When it comes to sports, I never really knew what I was good at. Because of my small and fragile build, I never had much chance to explore different sports. Even simple activities like running can leave me struggling to breathe, possibly because I had asthma when I was younger. I also don't want to push myself to the point of exhaustion. Still, there is one sport that has always caught my interest: badminton. At first, I thought badminton was simple. You just hit the shuttlecock back and forth with a racket. But after seeing how quick professional athletes move, I realized that there is much more to the sport than I had imagined. We were only taught the basics of badminton at school, and I never realized how physically demanding and challenging it could be. Despite that, I still wanted to try learning it. I have only played casually with my sisters, usually without a net, and I have never played a proper game. Because of that, I sometimes wonder what might have happened if I had been given more opportunities to explore different sports when I was younger. Maybe I could have discovered whether badminton was something I was actually good at—or perhaps found another sport that suited me. I may never know, but I think part of me will always be curious about the things I could have discovered if I had been given the chance to try.

Maybe these interests were never about becoming talented at something. They were simply things I wanted to experience and discover for myself. I may never know how far I could have gone, but they remain as reminders of the things I once wanted to try and the possibilities I never got to explore.

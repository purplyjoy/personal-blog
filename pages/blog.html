Regular Cycle

I noticed that my monthly period had became more regular during summer break. For the past few months, my period has consistently started during the first week of the month. It wasn't like this before. My period used to come on different dates, making it difficult for me to predict when it would start. I think the change might have something to do with having less stress during the break. There were no school projects to worry about, no deadlines to chase, and no schoolwork constantly on my mind. For once, I could just enjoy my time without thinking about what I needed to finish first.

Before, it was difficult to monitor when my period would come because of how inconsistent it was. When it was supposed to start on a specific day, it sometimes wouldn't. There could be a delay for a few days or even a week. Sometimes, my period would suddenly come when I was already outside the house, which could be really inconvenient. Even though I tracked my cycle, there was always a chance that my period wouldn't follow the schedule I expected. But since summer break started, things have been different. My period has consistently started during the first week of the month, making it much easier for me to keep track of it, I can't say for sure the stress was the reason for the change, but I can't help but notice the timing. When the academic year ended, I also felt less pressured and had fewer things to worry about. Maybe having some time to rest did make a difference.

It's a small thing, but I appreciate having a little more predictability when it comes to my period. I hope things stay this way even when school starts again. I know the stress of projects, deadlines, and everything else will come back, but I hope my body can keep up with me this time.

Memory Keeping

Memorable moments in life are meant to be treasured and remembered. We never know what experiences await us in the future, but it is important to value what we have already encountered. Even the simple things we accomplish help shape who we are today and who we will become in the future.

My family used to live in a traditional nipa hut, a small house that could easily be swept away by a strong typhoon. So, whenever there is a coming disaster we all pack our things and wrap them in plastic. I remember how devastated I was when I forgot about my photo album from Grade 7. It was soaked in water and all the photos I've kept were all ruined. I really loved that photo album because it was a gift from my friend when we had our own exchange gift on Christmas. That's when I became more sentimental about the gifts I received because I don't know when I'll lose them. Since then, I always keep all the important things in a storage box and make sure it will not be wet by the rain. In that box, all the memorable things in my life are kept. I have a new photo album where I will make sure from now on not to get soaked in water. I also kept all the letters I received from my friends. Some of the notebooks and papers I used during high school are also in there. Everything that I want to keep are piled up, including my diaries I used back from my elementary and high school days.

While I was looking through that storage box, I saw and reread all the things I had done before. I realized that I used to be such an enthusiastic person, and all those happy moments were written in my diary. Even though I no longer write in a physical notebook, I still keep my thoughts in the notes on my phone. Reading them brought back so many memories and allowed me to feel the same emotions I felt during those times. I truly loved being a child, and I will always cherish that phase of my life.

Another way I preserve memories is through Google Drive, where I upload photos and videos that I want to keep for the long term. I organize everything into separate folders, so I always know exactly where to find the photos or videos I want to look at and remember. Every small event in my life, no matter how ordinary it may seem, becomes meaningful when I capture it, and I make sure to upload it to Google Drive. This way, I can revisit those moments anytime and reminisce about the experiences, the people I shared them with, and the feelings I had at that time. Over the years, my collection has grown into a personal archive of memories— a digital photo album that reminds me of my journey, my growth, and the countless moments that have shaped who I am today.

Everything I have experienced in life has taught me valuable lessons and helped me grow into the person I am today and who I will become in the future. The memories I have kept will always serve as reminders of my journey and motivate me to keep working hard toward my dreams. I have set a goal of collecting even more memories and organizing them into carefully arranged photo albums. Each memory will have its own place and in their respective folders for different types of memories, so each album is organized based on what the photos are about, making it easier to revisit and reflect on the moments I've captured. These memories will not only preserve my past but also inspire me to pursue my dreams and embrace new experiences that I have longed for, reminding me that every step of my journey matters.

Maybe I Like the Idea of Him

Someone has been occupying a little too much space in my thoughts lately. For a while, I was seriously considering confessing to him. I even wrote a confession letter. But after finishing it, I started wondering what I was actually going to do after sending it. What happens if he rejects me? What happens if he doesn't? I don't really know his personality, his habits, or what he's like outside of the little things I've seen online. And maybe that is exactly why I like him. There is so much I don't know about him, and somehow, that uncertainty makes me curious. I find myself imagining what it would be like to actually know him. How we would have longer conversations, learn about the things he likes, hear about his life, and see what kind of person he really is. I even wonder about the smallest things, like how he talks in person or whether he would be fun to talk to when we actually have a proper conversation. I think I have filled in many of those unknown parts with my own imagination.

Sometimes I imagine us becoming a couple, even though we haven't really gotten to know each other yet. My imagination can go surprisingly far, too. I've thought about what it would be like if we ended up together, how we would deal with the distance since we live in different regions, and even where we might actually settle. It sounds ridiculous when I actually put it into words, especially considering how little I know about him. Maybe I'm not really in love with him. Maybe I'm attached to the possibility of him. I've created this version of him in my head based on the little things I know, the things I wonder about, and all the scenarios I imagine. I probably know more about the person I've created in my head than the actual person himself.

And yet, I still genuinely want to know him. I want to know what he's like when he's comfortable around someone. I want to know his hobbies, what makes him laugh, what kind of person he is, and all those ordinary details that you only learn when you actually spend time with someone. I want to know what if feels like to have a real conversation with him instead of getting a few short replies here and there. Of course, that also makes me overthink everything. Sometimes he replies with very short messages, or even nothing, and I start wondering if he just isn't interested in talking to me. Then I see him typing normally somewhere else and suddenly I'm analyzing his typing like it contains some hidden message. Maybe it means nothing at all. But when you like someone, apparently even when the way they type can become something worth thinking about. The difficult part is that I don't even know if he likes me, and I'm starting to realize that I might be setting myself up to be disappointed. It's hard to expect someone to like you when you barely talk to each other in the first place.

So, maybe I should stop trying to turn this into something before there is actually something there. I don't know if I like him because I genuinely have feelings for him or because I have too much time to think about him. Maybe it's a little bit of both. Having an online crush makes it easy to fill the silence with imagination. You can wonder about someone endlessly without ever having to confront the reality of who they really are. For now, I think I'd rather get to know him first, if I even get the chance. I'll just talk to him like I used to and let the waves take us wherever they're meant to, whether that to a relationship or nowhere at all. Either way, I know I'll eventually get over this.

Forgotten Interests

Everyone has things they are interested in. Some people discover their interests early and turn them into hobbies, skills, or even careers. Others try different things until they find something that feels right for them. But not everyone gets the chance to explore everything they are curious about. Sometimes, an interest is left behind because of circumstances. Maybe there wasn't enough money, time, support, or opportunity. Sometimes, we simply grow older and become occupied with other things. What once excited us slowly becomes something we no longer talk about or think about.

I have had many interests like that—things I wanted to try, experience, or become good at, but eventually stopped pursuing. There were not necessarily things I was skilled at. I never got far enough to find out. They were simply things that caught my attention and made me curious about what I could do. But because of different circumstances, many of them remained only as things I wanted to try.

When I had a bicycle as a kid and learned how to ride it, I knew I would have loved cycling. I imagined myself riding with other people, joining biking activities, and perhaps even participating in biking events someday. The Child I Was

There was a time when all I wanted was to grow up. I wished I could fast-forward into the future, skip waiting, and become the adult I imagined. I remember how I used to spend my days playing from morning until night. Most of my childhood was spent outside with friends, making our own adventures. I often go to the rice fields not too far from our home. We would walk there under the bright afternoon sun, picking fruits and flowers along the way. Sometimes, we'd even come home carrying a bag full of mangoes. Those simple trips became some of my favorite childhood memories. That was our way of spending time together, creating fun out of the ordinary.

I'm not just all about playing games though. I'm was also a good student. Back in elementary school, I was a consistent honor student. I was one of the top students in our batch, and I was actively involved in school organizations, activities, and competitions. I was chosen as the representative of our grade level for a storytelling competition. I had to reenact an entire story all by myself, playing not only the main character but also the supporting characters. It was challenging, but somehow, I pulled it off. My performance was apparently amazing—as everyone told me afterward—and I ended up winning the competition. I knew how things worked, I was comfortable with my environment, and I knew what kind of student I was. Then suddenly, I entered a completely different world. There will be times when things change, when you struggle to adjust, and when you don't perform the way you used to. And that's okay. Being an active and competitive student became such an important part of my childhood. Through those experiences, I gained more than medals, certificates, and recognition. I gained memories, confidence, knowledge, friendships, and values that have become a part of who I am.

Childhood was a chapter I once wanted to leave behind, only to realize later how much I would miss it. I could spend an entire day playing outside, joining activities, competing in contests, and talking to people without giving it much thought. Now, I feel like I've lost so much of that energy. I'm much quieter, more hesitant, and far less active than I was when I was a kid. Even the student I used to be feels like someone from a different time. Now, I don't even get honors anymore, I rarely participate in school events, and I find myself avoiding the spotlight rather than stepping into it. I even became afraid of being in front of people, which feels almost ironic when I remember how I used to stand onstage and perform without hesitation.

It's strange how things can turn completely opposite from what they used to be. The energetic child became a quieter adult, the competitive student became someone who no longer participates as much, and the girl who eagerly joined school activities became someone who would rather stay in the background. Maybe growing up isn't about remaining the same person we were as children. People change, and sometimes, along the way, we lose parts of ourselves that we once took for granted. But perhaps I don't have to become exactly who I was again. I can carry a piece of that child with me—the curiosity, confidence, energy, and ability to find joy in ordinary things. I may not be the same carefree and competitive kid I once was, but she is still a part of me, and maybe someday, I can slowly find some of her again.
